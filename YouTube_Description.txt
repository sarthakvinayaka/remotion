Microservices vs Platform Engineering: why "just split it into services" stops working at scale, and what actually fixes it.

Microservices sound great at first — split the app into smaller pieces, let teams move faster, ship independently. But give it a few months, and most companies hit the same wall: the services are fine, it's everything AROUND them that starts to hurt. Deployments get messy, observability is inconsistent, and every team ends up solving the same infrastructure problem from scratch.

At 10 services, you're not just building 10 services — you're building 10 services AND 10 copies of the same CI/CD, Docker, Kubernetes, logging, monitoring, alerts, secrets management, and API gateway setup. Every team now has to know how to deploy, monitor, secure, debug, and recover. The platform quietly becomes the product, even though nobody meant for that to happen.

This is exactly where platform engineering comes in. Instead of every team reinventing the same setup, one platform team builds a single standard path — a "paved road" with service templates, automated deployments, standard logging and monitoring, secrets management, a service catalog, and self-service infrastructure. Microservices give you the building blocks. Platform engineering gives you the paved road.
